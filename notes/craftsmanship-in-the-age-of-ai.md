AI lowers the cost of implementation. One person can build across a far wider range than before, and the number of possible outputs explodes.

The bottleneck then moves from production to judgment.

```text
Lower implementation costs
→ More output
→ Selection and verification become the bottleneck
→ Human judgment becomes more valuable
```

Three abilities matter here.

- **Taste** — recognizing what is good
- **Craftsmanship** — knowing how to reach a good result
- **Judgment** — deciding what to keep and what to discard

AI is good at adding things. A skilled human keeps only what is essential. This is why good code, products, organizations, and strategies often appear simple.

The standard for building an organization changes as well.

> How many people do we need?

matters less than

> How many independent human judgments do we need?

A founder who understands both product and technology can use AI to close the loop of `idea → implementation → use → revision` directly. This makes it possible for a small team to build a strong product.

> **In the age of AI, competitive advantage lies not in producing more, but in the craftsmanship to know what to build and what to discard.**

Good minimalism is not about making less.

It is what happens when someone capable of building complexity leaves only what is essential.
