Technology by itself is only a possibility. It gains value only when someone actually uses it.

But what is technically good and what is good for people to use are not the same. Better performance alone does not change human behavior.

Researchers tend to see problems that are novel and difficult. Users see how easily and reliably their own problems are solved.

This often creates a gap between the problems researchers consider important and the problems people actually care about. The more we speak only among PhDs, the harder that gap becomes to see.

Getting someone to pay is harder still. Being useful is not enough. There must be a clear reason that makes going without the product more costly than paying for it.

Building good technology and building important technology are therefore different things. If a technology does not change human choice, its impact remains limited, however impressive it may be technically.

Research asks what can be built. People ask why they should use it. Business asks why they should pay for it.
