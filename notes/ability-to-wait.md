I want to succeed. I have never imagined life after success, nor do I know whether it would make me happy. Yet if asked what I want now, I would still answer: success.

Success means producing a result different from others. Becoming better may be growth, but success exists only in comparison.

A different outcome requires a difference. If we see the same things and judge them in the same way, our results are unlikely to differ.

If that difference can be copied, success will not last. What was exceptional soon becomes ordinary, and when the advantage disappears, the result returns to the mean.

We therefore need a difference that is difficult to copy. Technology and knowledge eventually spread, but an ability that is painful to imitate does not spread easily.

The ability to act against human nature is difficult to copy. Knowing what is right is not the same as enduring fear and desire long enough to act on it.

One such ability is waiting. It is refusing ambiguous opportunities, preserving time and attention, and moving decisively when a truly asymmetric opportunity arrives.
